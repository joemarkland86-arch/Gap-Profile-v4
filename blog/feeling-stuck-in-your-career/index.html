Feeling stuck in your career? What's actually in the way

Joe Markland, career coach

If you're feeling stuck in your career, you've probably had plenty of advice already. Update your CV. Network more. Have a proper think about what you want. You probably know most of it. You're just not doing it.

The short answer: Knowing the next step doesn't explain why you're not taking it. You might doubt you can do it, worry about what could go wrong, question whether it suits you, or have no time or energy left. Sometimes the obstacle is outside your control. Start by checking what applies to the particular move you're considering.

Four things that can sit behind a stuck feeling

They can overlap, and they can change depending on the step you're facing.

You doubt you can do it. Someone praises your work and, before they've finished the sentence, you've explained it away. The timing was good. The team helped. Anyone could have done it. Sometimes that's fair. Sometimes it leaves out your part.

The downside feels too big. You research the move in detail, then find a new reason why now isn't quite the right time. Putting it off can bring relief, while leaving your concern untested. Sometimes waiting is sensible, especially when money or job security is at stake.

It doesn't feel like you. The move makes sense on paper. You just can't picture yourself doing it. That might mean it's wrong for you, or it might mean you've never tried it.

You've nothing left. You care about your career, but by the time work, the house and everything else is sorted, there's little time or energy for it. A big plan sits in your head unused.

Try this before reading another career article

Pick one step you've been putting off. Not your entire career. Just one move you can examine properly.

  1. What exactly am I putting off? 'Sort my career out' is hard to act on. 'Ask my manager for 20 minutes about leading the next project' is something you can actually do.
  2. What happens when I picture doing it? Am I doubting a skill, worried about the consequences, unsure it fits, or short of time and support? What else could be going on?
  3. What would help me decide? Perhaps it's one question to ask, a requirement to check, a small test, or a safeguard to put in place. Pausing can be a sensible decision too.

Whatever you notice, don't force it into one of the four explanations. Limited opportunities, unfair treatment and a move that genuinely isn't right for you deserve to be taken seriously.
